# Decision log

Decisions made while designing and building the prototype (Oct 25–26, 2026), with the reasoning.
"Randy" = Randy's call. "Accepted" = suggested during the design session and Randy agreed.

| # | Decision | Why | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Prototype Stage 1** (get to indication-ready) rather than the proposal pack | It's the gate: if an agent has to hand-collect five fields for 200 renewals, nothing downstream happens. Also shows AI judgment (confidence, review), not just text generation. | Randy |
| 2 | **Build Stage 1 as an LLM wiki** (Karpathy pattern: raw → wiki → schema) | Renewals repeat every year, so a client wiki compounds: year two only ingests what changed. Industry pages are shared across every client in that industry. | Randy |
| 3 | **Revenue first; stock price dropped** | Randy proposed checking public/private status, stock price and FTEs. Stock price and market cap don't feed an indication, and nearly all targets are private. Kept: a cheap "is it public?" branch (exact revenue from a 10-K) and headcount-based estimation for private firms. | Accepted |
| 4 | **Revenue waterfall:** agency's own records → public filings → benchmark estimate | The client is renewing other business lines, so the agency may already have revenue (rating basis) or payroll/headcount (workers' comp). External estimation is the fallback and a cross-check ("AMS says $2M, signals say $8M"). | Accepted |
| 5 | Estimates are **ranges with confidence and a stated method**, never bare points | Revenue is the hard field for private SMBs; reviewers need to see why a number is what it is. | Accepted |
| 6 | **Entity resolution is human-in-the-loop** | Legal name vs DBA vs same-name businesses in other states. Quoting the wrong company is the worst failure. The model scores candidates; a person confirms. | Randy |
| 7 | **No domain security scan; an exposure estimate instead** | Not possible in this project. Estimate a rough exposure from employee count (email accounts = phishing doors), data sensitivity, and public DNS email settings (SPF/DMARC are a free DNS lookup, not a scan). Agents can adjust it with a slider. | Randy (+ DNS signal accepted) |
| 8 | **Skipped for now:** appetite/class-code filter, check for existing BOP cyber endorsement, golden-dataset accuracy eval, privacy/compliance deep-dive, cost and latency per enrichment | Scope. Listed as next steps and in the write-up. | Randy |
| 9 | **Stage 2 = a digital HTML report** rendered from the wiki, with adjustable estimates; AI recommends, any agent can adjust | Owners won't read a PDF of jargon; agents need to correct estimates without asking anyone. | Randy |
| 10 | **Carrier numbers are locked;** estimates are adjustable | Changing a premium, limit or deductible misrepresents the carrier. If revenue moves beyond ±15% of what the carrier priced on, the report says "indication needs refresh" instead of recalculating. | Accepted |
| 11 | **Every published/sent version is version-controlled, with who moved which slider** (lock → submit → send) | Audit trail for compliance; overrides become labeled data showing where the AI is consistently wrong. | Randy |
| 12 | **Postgres owns the numbers; the wiki owns the evidence** | Numbers must be consistent and never AI-generated. The AI writes prose and reasoning; figures flow DB → report only. | Randy |
| 13 | **Agent mode vs client mode** of the same report | Agent sees sliders, AI recommendations, confidence. Client sees only a locked, submitted version. | Accepted |
| 14 | **Stage 3 = an Agent Brief** (pitch, objections, escalation triggers, specimen "where to find it" map, application preview) | Equip the producer without them reading policy forms. Objection answers come from a CPL-approved library; AI selects and tailors, doesn't invent. | Accepted |
| 15 | **Stage 3 includes a Claude skill to ask about the client** ("Ask about this client") | Agents can query the wiki and DB before a meeting. Read-only; numbers from the DB, reasons from the wiki; coverage questions escalate to the CPL. | Randy |
| 16 | **Stage 4 = a human phone Q&A, captured with Wispr Flow** | AI does the paperwork; the agent has a conversation. The question list covers only what the wiki doesn't already know, in plain language. Transcript goes into the wiki; answers into the DB. More personal than a form. | Randy |
| 17 | Stage 4 guardrails: **client e-signs the application**, **hedged answers never become "yes,"** **recording consent at the start of the call** | The application is a legal representation; "we mostly use MFA" can void a claim later; some states require all-party consent. | Accepted |
| 18 | **One client, built deep:** wiki + Postgres + skills + Wispr Flow + simple React front end | Randy chose depth on one end-to-end slice over a broad, shallow demo. The renewal queue (many clients) is shown as a "how this scales" slide. | Randy |
| 19 | ~~**Fictional client, real-world numbers**~~ (superseded by #45) | Avoids presenting a real company's estimated security weaknesses to execs. Premiums, revenue benchmarks and claim costs are real published figures; client, agency, carriers and transcript are invented. | Randy |
| 20 | **Runs on Randy's Mac, everything in the project folder, no Docker** | Postgres data directory lives in `db/pgdata`; Python managed with uv; the wiki is an Obsidian vault. | Randy |
| 21 | **Raw sources stored as Markdown** (JSON inside a code block) with wikilink citations | Obsidian hides .json files; Markdown makes every source visible, linkable, and part of the graph. | Accepted |
| 22 | **Intake happens before the client pack, as a human-in-the-loop step** (Wispr Flow calls + the intake answers page) | Randy, Oct 26: gather information at intake first, then build the pack from it. The agent has the conversation; AI does the paperwork. | Randy |
| 23 | **Intake can take several calls; answers merge across calls** | Owners rarely know everything on one call ("I'd have to check with IT"). A later call fills gaps; it never silently overwrites an answer an agent confirmed. | Randy |
| 24 | **The client pack proceeds with incomplete intake** | Waiting for every answer stalls the renewal window. Open items show as "to confirm" in the pack and are closed before the application. | Randy |
| 25 | **AI gathers industry research from the internet for the pack** (real incidents, regulation) | Makes "could this happen to us?" concrete with cited, recent events. Stored in the wiki (`raw/research/`), every item with URL and date, reviewed by a person; figures are quoted from the source, not generated. | Randy |
| 26 | **The client pack is a standalone HTML file on disk**: `reports/<slug>/current.html` for the working draft, `v<N>.html` per submitted version | Randy asked where the HTML report lives. A file can be opened, emailed or printed without the app; submitted versions never change. | Accepted |
| 27 | **Intake answers lower the exposure score** only on a clear "yes" (MFA, tested backups, EDR, payment call-backs) | Rewards confirmed controls; partial or hedged answers earn nothing. | Accepted |
| 28 | **One intake, two inputs: live taps + the recording** (merged from the parallel intake build) | The agent taps answers on the Live call screen while talking; the Wispr Flow recording is the evidence. Where they disagree it's a CONFLICT for the agent to resolve; the recording wins by default. | Randy |
| 29 | **The full intake question bank (identity, 20 exposure questions, walkthrough checks) is the one source** for the call screen, extraction, the intake wiki page, and the application | One list; `app_keys` map intake items to carrier application questions, so one conversation fills both. | Randy |
| 30 | **Live taps are saved to the database, not the browser** | Replaces "copy answers for ingest": taps survive a refresh, are attributed to the agent, and feed the conflict check automatically. | Accepted |
| 31 | **Confirming an intake answer confirms the application questions it fills**; confirmed revenue/records update the report (logged) | One confirmation per fact instead of two. | Accepted |
| 32 | **Re-ingesting an older call never overwrites a newer call's answer**; a hedged "yes" earns no exposure credit | Merge order follows when calls were ingested; credits only for clean answers. | Accepted |
| 33 | **Walkthrough figures the prototype doesn't model show as labelled placeholders** (BI loss, ransom range, notification cost, etc.); figures the DB holds (waiting period, incident response cost, social-engineering sublimit, recommended limit) are filled | Never estimate a number on the call. The limit-sizing model is the next build. | Accepted |
| 34 | **Postgres is bundled in the project** (the `pgserver` package installs the server into `.venv` via uv) | Randy wanted Postgres housed only in the project: no Homebrew, no system install. Data stays in `db/pgdata`; the script still accepts another Postgres via `PG_BIN`. | Randy |
| 35 | **Two terminals: backend (uvicorn) + frontend (Vite dev server)**; the built `web/dist` is also served by the API | Randy will keep editing the front end, so hot reload matters; `web/dist` still allows a one-command demo. | Randy |
| 36 | **Node is bundled in the project too** (`nodejs-wheel` in `.venv`, run as `uv run npm`) | Same rule as Postgres: nothing installed system-wide; uv is the only prerequisite. | Randy |

| 37 | **Stage 1 is the agent ↔ carrier exchange, not only enrichment**: requests out, replies in, both stored | Randy, Oct 26: an indication comes *from* a carrier, so Stage 1 ends when the replies are in and understood, not when the five fields are filled. | Randy |
| 38 | **Replies are ingested from any channel** (PDF letter, email, underwriter call captured with Wispr Flow) into one pipeline; each reply is an immutable raw source | Carriers answer however they answer. Source-agnostic ingest keeps one history. | Randy (channel-agnostic design accepted) |
| 39 | **History, never overwrite:** every reply appends a version per carrier + option, with a diff and the adjustments it requires | Randy: terms change over time; keep the history and show what needs to change. | Randy |
| 40 | **Two options requested from every carrier: $1M / $10K and $2M / $25K** (replaces three tiers) | Comparable across carriers; matches the client pack's two columns. | Randy |
| 41 | **Verbal numbers are stored but never client-facing**; the written reply supersedes them | "Probably around twenty-three hundred" is a lead, not a price. | Accepted |
| 42 | **Extracted figures must appear verbatim in the source**; anything that doesn't (shorthand, calculated dates, wrong client name) waits for a person. Values are locked by a database trigger | The model reads the letter; the carrier is the only origin of a number. Review by exception: most replies pass untouched. | Accepted |
| 43 | **Actions come from fixed rules, not the model** (price change, new condition checked against the intake, decline, verbal-only, figure to confirm; alerts for expiry and revenue drift) | Predictable and explainable; the agent sees exactly why something is on the list. | Accepted |
| 44 | **The demo seed sends requests before entity/revenue review, and says so**; the app's re-request is gated on that review | Keeps the live "confirm the entity" moment in the demo without hiding the shortcut. | Accepted |

| 45 | **Real client: Sloan's Lake Dental (Sage Dentistry, PLLC, Denver)**, using only public data; agency records, calls and carrier replies stay simulated and labeled | Randy, Oct 26: it's an interview demo, so make it as real as possible: real records, real threats. Public records also make a better Stage 1 story than invented ones (a delinquent entity with the practice's own name, a stale directory address, no DMARC). Carriers stay fictional because a made-up quote on a real carrier's letterhead would be a fake document; simulated calls use roles, not real staff names, so no one is quoted saying something they didn't. | Randy (simulation boundaries accepted) |
| 46 | **Entity resolution weighs registry status**: a non-good-standing entity's score is halved | The real registry has "Sloans Lake Dental PLLC" (delinquent) at the same address as the right entity. Name + address alone would pick it. | Accepted |
| 47 | **Headcount with no public signal is an explicit estimate** (3 dentists × ~4 support staff, an unsourced assumption), low confidence, flagged | Nothing public gives this practice's headcount; the estimate says so instead of borrowing false precision. | Accepted |

| 48 | *(Superseded by #55)* **Stage 1 is one screen**: step A (five fields) and step B (carrier exchange) together; no separate 1b tab | Randy, Oct 26: the brief's Stage 1 is "get indications"; the carrier exchange is part of it, not a stage of its own. | Randy |
| 49 | **Three ways to bring in a carrier reply: conversation (Wispr Flow transcript), clipboard, API** (plus files dropped in the wiki) | Randy: we don't know how carriers deliver indications to UKON today, so support all of them and record which one each reply used. API replies are structured and skip the model; the API is simulated. | Randy |
| 50 | **All five fields are editable (including legal name), and every value records its source** (pipeline, conversation, clipboard, API, typed) with a reference and who entered it | Randy: the agent must be able to fix anything, and the provenance is part of the record; it shows on the field and in the audit trail. Carrier figures stay locked (#10). | Randy |

| 51 | **Everything is editable in the mockup**: all fields (including dentists and the published benchmarks) and carrier figures. Carrier edits add a new version marked "edited by", confirmed by the editor, with source and note; the earlier version stays | Randy, Oct 26: it's an interview mockup; he needs to change any number while presenting. Supersedes #10's "carrier numbers locked" for the UI; history and attribution still hold. | Randy |
| 52 | **The API refuses an out-of-date database** (schema version check) with "reload: run pipeline.run_all" | A missing column made "Confirm" silently do nothing after an update; the app now says what to do, and buttons show errors instead of swallowing them. | Accepted |

| 53 | *(Superseded by #55)* **Stage 1's screen is the five fields only; the carrier-exchange screen (step B) is removed from the UI** | Randy, Oct 26: not needed for the demo. Indications are still loaded by the pipeline so the client pack has prices; the backend (versions, checks, paste/API ingest) stays in the code, unused by the UI. | Randy |

| 54 | **Cleanup: code for the removed carrier-exchange screen deleted** (paste / conversation / API inputs, carrier-figure editing, review and action endpoints, the retired Quillfeather client, old caches). Under Git from here | Randy, Oct 26: changes were getting slow. The pipeline still ingests carrier reply files for the client pack's prices; decisions #49 and #51's carrier editing are withdrawn with the screen. | Randy |

| 55 | **Five stages: UKON's Stage 1 is split into 1 · Confirm data and 2 · Get indications**; stages 3–5 are UKON's 2–4. Stage 2 gets its own screen again (supersedes #48 and #53): send to chosen carriers (gated on Stage 1), read replies in from the inbox, every carrier's versions read-only, check a flagged figure, close follow-ups with a note. The detailed security/application questions move from a pre-pack "intake" to Stage 5; Stage 1 shows only the five fields (estimates and benchmarks move to Stage 3). Each stage's status is derived from the data (`pipeline/stages.py`), not set by hand | Randy, Oct 26: the two halves of "get indications" have different owners and failure modes, and a person's approval belongs between them before client data leaves the agency. Asking security questions before the owner has seen a price adds friction. The demo seed still runs call 1 before the pack (labelled) so the pack shows answers so far | Randy |

## Things flagged but not yet resolved

- Whether Wispr Flow captures **phone calls** or only meetings and dictation. The design says
  "call capture (e.g., Wispr Flow)" so it doesn't depend on one tool. Verify before the demo.
- Whether carriers accept a **revenue band** for an indication (vs a point value).
- How indications come back today: **API vs portal vs email vs phone.** The ingest is channel-agnostic; the mix decides how much is automatic. Ask account managers where an indication physically arrives.
- The **limit-sizing model** behind the walkthrough (ten formulas in `docs/client-intake.md`) needs sourced defaults before its figures can be shown.
