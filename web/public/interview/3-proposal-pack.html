Deliverable 03

Client proposal pack

The owner isn't technical and doesn't understand cyber insurance. So the pack is built around the four questions they actually ask, in the order they ask them, and it leads with price. Many owners will read only the first section.

What the owner wants to know, in order

1What does it cost?

Price first. Burying it behind education reads as a sales pitch.

2Could this really happen to me?

Stories from their own industry, and real recent incidents with sources.

3What do I get?

Plain-language coverage, including what's not covered.

4What do I do next?

Few steps, no surprises at signing.

At a glance

One or two sentences on why it matters to this business, then the two requested options side by side: monthly and annual estimated premium, limit, deductible, and the "tricked into sending money" limit. "Most dental offices choose this" marks the $1M option.

Why: the owner decides here. The money-transfer limit is called out because owners assume it's fully covered.

2

What you've told us so far

Each security topic from the call (phone codes on email, backups, call-backs on payment changes, staff training…) marked In place, Partly, Not yet, or To confirm.

Why: it shows we listened, and it previews what the insurer will ask, so nothing is a surprise at signing.

Call answers
3

What could happen to a practice like yours

First, what a day of keeping the practice running costs: operating costs (OPEX) of $3,900–$4,200 a day, and staff wages alone of $1,910 a day ($336 per working hour), each with its written formula. Then three short industry stories, such as being locked out of the schedule, a fake lab invoice, or a breach letter. Each story says it's an example and links the real incidents behind it; each cost says how it's worked out and where it comes from. The fake-invoice card adds what one redirected paycheck ($2,240) and one whole pay run ($26,800) would be.

Why: "we're too small to be a target" is the most common objection. A story from their own world answers it better than a statistic, and owners trust a number they can check: their own operating costs make the risk concrete, and every figure shows its formula and source.

Operating costs, staff wages, payrollStoriesBenchmark costs
4

Your exposure snapshot

A Lower / Moderate / Elevated meter with plain reasons ("12 email logins a criminal can try", "no DMARC record") and which unconfirmed steps would lower it.

Why: it makes the risk specific and gives the owner something they can act on. It's labelled as an estimate, not a security rating.

ExplainerScore
5

Practices like yours, in the news

Real, recent incidents in the industry, each with its source and date and one line on why it matters to this client.

Why: real events earn trust in a way hypotheticals can't. Every item needs a URL and date; nothing is invented.

Researched, cited, reviewed
6

What it pays for

Six coverage areas in plain language, what isn't covered, and the waiting period before lost income is paid.

Why: overstating coverage is the compliance risk, so this text only comes from the approved library.

Two views of the same pack

Agent view

  • Sliders for revenue, employees, exposure, records and average staff pay, with the starting value (the AI's, or the national average for staff pay) shown next to the current one
  • Carrier picker; carrier premiums, limits and deductibles are locked
  • "Indication needs refresh" if revenue moves more than 15% from what carriers priced on
  • Every change records who, when, old → new, and why
  • Undo a submit before it's sent: the version is kept as withdrawn, editing continues in a new draft
  • A "Locked versions" dropdown opens any earlier version read-only

Client view

  • Only the latest submitted version, so the client never sees an unreviewed draft
  • No sliders and no "AI thinks"
  • Sent versions never change; editing creates a new one
