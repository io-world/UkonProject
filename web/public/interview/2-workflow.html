Workflow map

From spotting an upcoming renewal to a bound policy. For each step: what AI does, where a person decides and why, and what could go wrong and how it gets caught.

10

places a person decides, from picking the renewal to the client's signature. Everything else is AI or automated.

0

figures written by AI. Premiums, limits and costs come from carriers and the database; AI prose fails validation if it contains a digit.

1

question nobody answers on the spot, AI or agent: "Would this be covered?" It goes to a Cyber Practice Leader.

Three kinds of work

AI

Reads, extracts and writes: a carrier PDF, answers from a call, the pack's prose. Flexible but can be wrong, so its output is always checked before it counts.

Automated

Fixed code: checking every figure against the source, version locks, the ±15% drift rule, follow-up rules, cost formulas, gates. The same input always gives the same output. It does the checking.

Person

Decides and confirms: the company match, sending to carriers, every application answer, the owner's choice, bind. Nothing a client sees counts until a person or the client says yes.

AI proposes, code verifies, a person decides.

Stage 5 · AI follow-up call

Gate

The agent approves it for this client, chooses which open questions it may ask, and who it calls.

AI

Calls the practice (typically the office manager) to ask only those factual application questions: MFA, backups, payment checks. It never sells, and never discusses price or coverage.

Automated

The recording comes in like any other call: every answer quoted with its line, hedges flagged, conflicts never auto-resolved.

Person

The agent confirms every answer in Check the recording, as now.

Why: the open questions are the part that stalls, and this takes the chasing off the agent without handing over the relationship.

After binding, the 30-60-90 plan and touchpoints carry the client to next year's renewal, which starts from this year's confirmed answers.

Why six stages, not four

UKON's Stage 1, "get indications," hides two jobs with different owners and failure modes: confirming the five facts (mostly already in the agency's file at renewal) and the round trip with carriers (requests out, replies in, versions, follow-ups). Splitting them puts a person's approval between the two, before client data leaves the agency. UKON's Stage 4 is split too: the questionnaire usually takes several calls and emails and is where deals stall, so it gets its own stage (5); the owner's choice, firm quote, bind and report are Stage 6. Stages 3 and 4 are UKON's 2 and 3. The detailed questions about the business and its security practices wait until after the pack: asking them before the owner has seen a price adds the friction the renewal window can't afford. The seeded demo runs one call early so the pack can show what the owner has said so far; in production the pack goes first.
