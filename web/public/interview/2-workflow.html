After binding, the 30-60-90 plan and touchpoints carry the client to next year's renewal, which starts from this year's confirmed answers.

Why six stages, not four

UKON's Stage 1, "get indications," hides two jobs with different owners and failure modes: confirming the five facts (mostly already in the agency's file at renewal) and the round trip with carriers (requests out, replies in, versions, follow-ups). Splitting them puts a person's approval between the two, before client data leaves the agency. UKON's Stage 4 is split too: the questionnaire usually takes several calls and emails and is where deals stall, so it gets its own stage (5); the owner's choice, firm quote, bind and report are Stage 6. Stages 3 and 4 are UKON's 2 and 3. The detailed questions about the business and its security practices wait until after the pack: asking them before the owner has seen a price adds the friction the renewal window can't afford. The seeded demo runs one call early so the pack can show what the owner has said so far; in production the pack goes first.
