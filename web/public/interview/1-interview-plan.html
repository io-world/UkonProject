Deliverable 01

Interview plan

Who I'd talk to at UKON before building, and what I'd ask. The rule for every question: only ask what only UKON can answer. Anything I can learn from the web, a carrier's public application or an AI isn't worth an interview slot. What's left is how the work actually moves today, UKON's own numbers, and the stories behind them.

What I don't know yet, in the order I'd find out

Every question below is tagged with the gap it closes.

AWhy the renewal window is missed

Matt, then two CPLs

Matt's brief says manual work and agents not raising cyber. Research also points to price, owners who think they're already covered, and carriers declining. I'd check this first against UKON's own numbers, because it decides what to build first.

BHow the work actually moves today

CPL, ops/submissions, agency success

Who does what, in which system, in what order, between agency, UKON and carriers. I'd watch one real submission end to end and replace the prototype's assumptions.

CWhat happens after bind

Service/claims

Who owns the client during the year, whether anyone checks security mid-term, what happens at an incident, and how the cyber policy renews.

The questions

In the order I'd ask them. Most are "walk me through the last one" or "show me": a real recent case beats an opinion about the average case.

#QuestionAskWhy I'm asking
1AYou wrote that the renewal moment gets missed because it's too much manual work. What is that based on: numbers, what agents tell you, or a specific deal?Matt (CEO)The whole design rests on his diagnosis. I want to know if it's measured or believed before building on it.
2APublic research points to a few causes besides manual work: price, owners believing another policy already covers them, and carriers requiring controls like MFA before they'll quote. Which of those do you actually see at UKON?
  • Price. Only 16.8% of SMEs worldwide have standalone cyber; GlobalData's lead analyst says "many smaller businesses have been priced out." GlobalData 2025 SME Survey, 2,054 SMEs in 14 countries incl. US, via Insurance Journal, Jun 30, 2026. A US broker: "it's just not something that they can afford right now." Insurance Journal, Mar 4, 2024
  • "Already covered." 35% of firms that decided against standalone cyber believe their wider commercial policy covers them; separately, 35% of firms that once had standalone cover no longer do. Coalition survey of 600 UK mid-market decision-makers, via Insurance Age, Nov 26, 2025
  • Carrier requirements. "Most insurers have begun requiring potential policyholders to have some level of MFA to provide a cyber insurance quote." Travelers, in Insurance Times (c. 2022)
  • None of this is US small-practice data; the best US evidence is a broker quote. UKON's own numbers would beat all of it.
Matt (CEO)Public research points to other causes. If they dominate, a faster workflow won't move the number.

Does the application happen before or after the client picks a carrier? Where is it wrong? 6BWalk me through three recent deals: one that bound, one that stalled and one that died. Who did what, when, and in which tool? Can I see what the agent sent you at the start, redacted?CPLReal cases show the actual workflow and where time goes, not the documented version.

9BWalk me through how one of your agencies runs a renewal at 120, 90, 60 and 30 days out. Who does what, and what can UKON see from the agency's system?Agency successShows whether renewals can be spotted automatically, and whether the work leaves a trail to mine.
10COnce a policy is bound, what are the touchpoints during the year that make the renewal easier, and who owns each one: the agent, UKON or the carrier? 11CWalk me through the last incident a UKON client reported, and any claim that was disputed. Was it because the application didn't match what was really in place?
  • Real example: Travelers sued to rescind a policy after a ransomware attack because the application overstated MFA. Travelers v. International Control Services, No. 22-cv-2145 (C.D. Ill., filed Jul 6, 2022). Farella Braun + Martel
Service / claimsShows the real cost of a wrong application answer, which sets how strict the questionnaire must be.

What I'd ask to see

Artifacts answer workflow questions faster and more honestly than descriptions.

  • A redacted submission, start to finish, for one recent client
  • A real indication from each main carrier (PDF, email or portal screen)
  • Last quarter's cyber submissions with outcome: indicated, declined, quoted, bound
  • Non-renewal and "quoted but didn't bind" lists, with any reason codes
  • One agency's renewal report from its management system
  • The carrier application(s) UKON uses most
  • How UKON and the agent are paid on cyber, new and renewal (if shareable)
  • The claims or incident procedure for a UKON-placed policy

Background: what the research already says (so we don't spend interview time on it)

Public sources answer the general questions. These are the competing explanations for gap A that the interviews above test against UKON's own data.

Which renewal do you mean, Matt? The renewal of the client's other policies (selling cyber for the first time), or the cyber policy's own renewal (keeping a client who already has it)? They're different problems with different causes.
