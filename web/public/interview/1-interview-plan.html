Deliverable 01

Interview plan

Who I'd talk to at UKON before building, and what I'd ask. The rule for every question: only ask what only UKON can answer. Anything I can learn from the web, a carrier's public application or an AI isn't worth an interview slot. What's left is how the work actually moves today, UKON's own numbers, and the stories behind them.

Three things I don't know yet

Every question below is tagged with the gap it closes.

AHow the work actually moves today

Who does what, in which system, in what order, between the agency, UKON and each carrier. The prototype assumes a lot here: how indications are requested and come back, what agents send, where time goes.

BWhat happens after a policy is bound

Who owns the client for the policy year, whether anyone checks security mid-term, what happens at an incident, and how the cyber policy's own renewal runs.

CWhy the renewal window is missed

Matt's brief says it's manual work and agents not raising cyber. Outside research points to other causes too: price, owners who think another policy covers them, carriers declining over missing controls. Which is true for UKON decides what to build first.

The order I'd run them

First · the problem

Matt, then two CPLs

Test the brief's explanation against UKON's own numbers and recent deals before anything else. Gap C first, because it decides whether this is a sales problem or a retention problem.

Second · the real workflow

Ops / submissions, agency success, engineering

Watch a real submission go from agency to carrier and back. Replace every assumption in the prototype with what actually happens.

Third · after bind

Service / claims, a CPL, compliance

Follow a bound policy through a year, an incident and a renewal.

Questions, by person at UKON

Most questions are "walk me through the last one" or "show me": a real recent case beats an opinion about the average case.

Matt (CEO) and the exec team

First

Why: the brief is his diagnosis. I want the evidence behind it, and his reaction to the other explanations, before building on it.

  1. CYou wrote that the renewal moment gets missed because getting a client to "protected" is too much manual work. What is that based on: numbers, agents telling you, a specific deal?Whether the core premise is measured or believed.
  2. COutside surveys blame price, owners thinking their package policy already covers cyber, and carriers declining clients without MFA or backups. Which of those do you see at UKON, and which don't you?Which causes are live for UKON's book.
  3. COf the cyber policies UKON places, roughly what share renews at year two? When one doesn't, do you know whether the client walked or the carrier declined?Whether the bigger problem is selling or keeping.
  4. CIf you had to pick: more agencies' clients buying cyber for the first time, or the clients who already bought keeping it?Where the first build should point.
  5. AHow does UKON earn on a cyber policy, and when: at bind, at each renewal? How does the agent earn?Who has the incentive to do each step.
  6. AWhat has UKON already tried to get agents to raise cyber, and what happened?What not to rebuild.

Changes: whether the first build is the sales path (what the prototype does) or a retention path, and which number we use to call it a success.

Cyber Practice Leaders (CPLs)

First

Why: they're inside every deal. They know where deals stall and what agents and owners really say.

  1. APick three recent deals: one that bound, one that stalled, one that died. Walk me through each, step by step: who did what, when, and in which tool.The real workflow, and where time actually goes.
  2. AWhen an agent brings you a client, what do they actually send? Can I see a real one, redacted?What data exists at the start, versus what the prototype assumes.
  3. AAt what point do you get pulled in: before the agent talks to the client, during, or after something goes wrong?Where review fits without adding a step.
  4. CTell me about the last client who said no. What reason did they give, and what do you think the real reason was?The gap between stated and real objections.
  5. CWhen a cyber client didn't renew, what happened? Did you know before it lapsed?Whether lapses are visible in time to act.
  6. BAfter bind, do you hear from the client or the agent again before renewal? About what?Whether anyone owns the policy year.

Changes: the review steps, the objection library, and whether UKON or the agent absorbs the work.

Ops / submissions / wholesale

Second

Why: they move submissions to carriers and indications back. The prototype guessed at all of it.

  1. AShow me a submission from start to finish on your screen: which systems, emails and carrier portals it touches, and what you re-type.The real path, and where automation pays.
  2. AFor each carrier you use for small-business cyber: how do you request an indication, how does it come back (portal, email, PDF, API, phone), and how long does it take?Replaces the prototype's assumption about carrier replies.
  3. AHow do you decide which carriers to send a client to? Is that written down anywhere?Whether carrier selection can be a rule.
  4. AOf last quarter's submissions, how many came back declined or asking for more information, and for what?Which data must be right first time.
  5. AWhen an indication comes back, who tells the agent, how, and how fast?The handoff the proposal pack depends on.
  6. CDo you track why quoted clients don't bind, and why cyber policies don't renew? Can I see the reason codes, or a list?The data that settles gap C.

Changes: which carriers to integrate first, what Stage 1 must collect, and the reason codes the product should capture from day one.

Agency success / account management

Second

Why: they see how agencies actually run renewals, which UKON's own systems don't show.

  1. AWalk me through how one of your agencies runs a renewal: 120, 90, 60, 30 days out. Who does what, and in which system?Where the renewal trigger should come from.
  2. AWhich agency management systems do your agencies use, and what can UKON see from them today?Whether the renewal queue can be automatic.
  3. AHow does an agency hand a client to UKON today: portal, email, phone?The entry point for Stage 1.
  4. CWhen you ask an agent why a renewing client wasn't offered cyber, what do they say? Does it differ between your best and worst agencies?Whether it's effort, confidence or incentive.

Changes: how the renewal queue is fed, and which agencies to pilot with.

Service / claims

Third

Why: nothing in the brief covers the year after bind, and I don't know who owns it.

  1. BAfter a cyber policy binds, who is the client's contact: the agent, UKON or the carrier? What does UKON do between bind and renewal?Who the post-bind experience is for.
  2. BWhich of your carriers monitor clients after bind, and do their alerts reach UKON or the agent, or only the client?Whether a mid-term check-in duplicates the carrier.
  3. BWalk me through the last incident a UKON-placed client reported: the first call, who helped, how long it took.What "protected" means in practice.
  4. BHave any claims been disputed or denied? Was it because the application didn't match what was really in place?How strict intake must be about hedged answers.
  5. BHow does the cyber policy's own renewal start, how early, and what has to be collected again?What carries forward a year, and what doesn't.
  6. BWhat happens mid-term when something changes: revenue jumps, a new location, a new IT provider?Whether the client's wiki needs a change log.

Changes: whether to build a mid-term check-in, and what the renewal of the cyber policy itself looks like.

Engineering / product

Second

Why: they know what data already exists and where it gets re-typed.

  1. AWhere do the client, submission, indication, policy and renewal live today? Where is the same data typed twice?The system of record the prototype should plug into.
  2. AWhich carrier connections exist today (API, portal, email), and which are on the roadmap?How much of the reply handling is real today.
  3. CCan you pull, for the last year: renewing clients offered cyber, quoted, bound, and still in force at year two?The funnel that answers gap C with numbers.

Changes: integration order and the success metric's baseline.

Compliance

Third

Why: UKON has already made decisions here that the prototype shouldn't contradict.

  1. AWho signs off client-facing material today, and what would they need to see before AI-written text reaches a client?The approval step for the proposal pack.
  2. ADoes UKON or its agencies record client calls today? What did you decide about consent?Whether call capture is allowed at all.
  3. BWho is responsible if an application answer turns out wrong after a claim: the agent, UKON or the client?How much confirmation each answer needs.

Changes: the sign-off step and the consent script.

Introductions I'd ask for

If UKON can connect me

Why: one hour with each closes the biggest assumptions faster than anything I can read.

  1. AA producer at a busy agency: "Show me your last ten renewals. Which ones did you offer cyber, and why not the rest?"Offer rate and the honest reasons.
  2. BAn underwriter on UKON's panel: "What do you do with a small-business policy between bind and renewal? What makes you decline at renewal?"Mid-term monitoring and renewal gates, for UKON's actual carriers.
  3. CA client who dropped cyber, and one who kept it: "What made you decide?"The retention story in their words.

What I'd ask to see

Artifacts answer workflow questions faster and more honestly than descriptions.

  • A redacted submission, start to finish, for one recent client
  • A real indication from each main carrier (PDF, email or portal screen)
  • Last quarter's cyber submissions with outcome: indicated, declined, quoted, bound
  • Non-renewal and "quoted but didn't bind" lists, with any reason codes
  • One agency's renewal report from its management system
  • The carrier application(s) UKON uses most
  • How UKON and the agent are paid on cyber, new and renewal (if shareable)
  • The claims or incident procedure for a UKON-placed policy

Background: what the research already says (so we don't spend interview time on it)

Public sources answer the general questions. These are the competing explanations for gap C that the interviews above test against UKON's own data.

Two problems hide in one question. The brief's "renewal window" is the renewal of the client's other policies, the moment to sell cyber for the first time. A client dropping a cyber policy at its own renewal is a retention problem, with different causes.
