Interview plan

Who I'd talk to at UKON before building, and what I'd ask. The rule for every question: only ask what only UKON can answer. Anything I can learn from the web, a carrier's public application or an AI isn't worth an interview slot. What's left is how the work actually moves today, UKON's own numbers, and the stories behind them.

Three things I don't know yet

Every question below is tagged with the gap it closes.

AHow the work actually moves today

Who does what, in which system, in what order, between the agency, UKON and each carrier. The prototype assumes a lot here: how indications are requested and come back, what agents send, where time goes.

BWhat happens after a policy is bound

Who owns the client for the policy year, whether anyone checks security mid-term, what happens at an incident, and how the cyber policy's own renewal runs.

CWhy the renewal window is missed

Matt's brief says it's manual work and agents not raising cyber. Outside research points to other causes too: price, owners who think another policy covers them, carriers declining over missing controls. Which is true for UKON decides what to build first.

The order I'd run them

First · the problem

Matt, then two CPLs

Test the brief's explanation against UKON's own numbers and recent deals before anything else. Gap C first, because it decides whether this is a sales problem or a retention problem.

Second · the real workflow

Ops / submissions, agency success

Watch a real submission go from agency to carrier and back. Replace every assumption in the prototype with what actually happens.

Third · after bind

Service / claims

Follow a bound policy through a year, an incident and a renewal.

What I'd ask to see

Artifacts answer workflow questions faster and more honestly than descriptions.

  • A redacted submission, start to finish, for one recent client
  • A real indication from each main carrier (PDF, email or portal screen)
  • Last quarter's cyber submissions with outcome: indicated, declined, quoted, bound
  • Non-renewal and "quoted but didn't bind" lists, with any reason codes
  • One agency's renewal report from its management system
  • The carrier application(s) UKON uses most
  • How UKON and the agent are paid on cyber, new and renewal (if shareable)
  • The claims or incident procedure for a UKON-placed policy

Background: what the research already says (so we don't spend interview time on it)

Public sources answer the general questions. These are the competing explanations for gap C that the interviews above test against UKON's own data.

Two problems hide in one question. The brief's "renewal window" is the renewal of the client's other policies, the moment to sell cyber for the first time. A client dropping a cyber policy at its own renewal is a retention problem, with different causes.
